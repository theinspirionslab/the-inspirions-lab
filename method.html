Method

No fixed recipe. A disciplined way of exploring.

The Inspirions Lab does not begin with a preselected solution or methodology. The question determines what we need to understand, and the situation determines which tools are useful.

1. Observe

Start with what is actually happening. What do people experience? What happens in practice? What information is available? Where does work flow — and where does it stop?

We distinguish observations from interpretations and interpretations from assumptions.

2. Frame the question

A problem statement is not necessarily the right question.

We ask what is really being constrained, who defines the problem, what has become normal and what might be missing from the current framing.

The anti-normality question is simple:

Why do we do it this way?

3. Understand the system

We map relationships rather than isolated components: people, purpose, information, technology, processes, resources, incentives, constraints and feedback.

Systems thinking helps reveal dependencies, loops and unintended consequences that are easy to miss when a problem is viewed in isolation.

4. Find the contradiction

Many difficult problems contain a tension: improving one characteristic makes another worse.

TRIZ provides a disciplined way to make such contradictions visible and to search beyond the usual compromise.

TRIZ is therefore one of the Lab's important thinking tools — not a mandatory recipe for every problem.

5. Explore possibilities

Once the system and its tensions are clearer, we deliberately widen the solution space.

This can involve analogy, biomimicry, scenario thinking, experiments, different organisational structures, technology, physical redesign or simply reframing the question.

The aim is not to produce more ideas. It is to discover possibilities that were invisible within the original framing.

6. Build the minimum necessary structure

Once a useful direction emerges, structure should support it without becoming the new constraint.

That may mean clearer information flows, a lightweight PMO layer, a different meeting structure, a prototype, a digital tool or a change in roles and responsibilities.

We favour the minimum structure needed to make purposeful action possible.

7. Experiment

Do not design everything in theory if a small experiment can reveal more.

Prototypes, pilots and short feedback cycles make assumptions visible and reduce the cost of being wrong.

8. Learn and adapt

The result of an experiment is not simply "success" or "failure". It is information.

What changed? What did we learn? Which assumption was wrong? What new question appeared?

The method therefore loops back:

Observe → Question → Understand → Explore → Experiment → Learn → Adapt

The tools are secondary

The Lab draws from different disciplines when they help answer the question.

These may include:

  • Systems thinking
  • TRIZ
  • Lean
  • Agile and iterative experimentation
  • Liberating Structures
  • Information and knowledge design
  • Network and ecosystem thinking
  • Biomimicry and cross-domain analogy
  • Artificial intelligence and digital tools

None of these is treated as universally correct. A tool is useful when it helps us see, decide, experiment or learn better.

Evidence before certainty

The Inspirions Lab also applies this discipline to its own thinking.

We try to distinguish:

  • what we directly observed;
  • what is supported by other evidence;
  • what has been reported to us;
  • what we interpret;
  • and what we currently hypothesise.

This matters because an attractive explanation can easily become mistaken for a fact when it is repeated often enough.

The method is itself an experiment

We do not expect this method to remain unchanged. New cases, evidence and counterexamples may change how we work.

The objective is not methodological purity. It is better understanding, better questions and more useful possibilities.

The method follows the question. The structure follows the purpose. The learning changes the method.

The Working Loop

Observe & Question

Start with what is happening. Question the framing, assumptions and constraints before reaching for solutions.

Explore & Experiment

Use different lenses to expose possibilities, then test promising directions rather than assuming they will work.

Learn & Adapt

Treat results as information. Keep what works, challenge what does not and let new evidence change the direction.
