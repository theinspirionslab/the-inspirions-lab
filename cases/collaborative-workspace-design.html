Collaborative Workspace Design

Designing lightweight structures for collaboration

A practical exploration of how digital workspaces can support clarity, ownership and learning without creating bureaucracy.

Why this case exists

Many organisations do not lack tools or information. The challenge is creating a shared environment where people know what matters, what happens next and what can be learned.

This case explores how a simple digital workspace can become a supporting layer between people, projects and purpose.

The challenge

Projects often create fragmented information: actions in one place, decisions somewhere else, and knowledge disappearing when people move on.

The challenge is not collecting more information, but creating the right structure to make information useful.

The approach

A lightweight workspace combines planning, communication, documentation and learning into one accessible environment.

  • One shared overview of priorities and progress.
  • Clear ownership of actions and decisions.
  • Reusable knowledge instead of lost experience.
  • A structure that supports collaboration rather than controls it.

The PMO+ perspective

A digital workspace should not replace human collaboration. It should create more space for meaningful conversations, problem solving and innovation.

The principle:

Structure should reduce friction, not increase administration.

First hypotheses

  • The best structure is the one people actually use.
  • Information should be captured once and serve multiple purposes.
  • A project environment should support learning, not only control.
Design Principles

Clarity

Creating shared understanding of goals, actions and decisions.

Connection

Connecting people, information and activities.

Learning

Turning daily work into reusable knowledge.
