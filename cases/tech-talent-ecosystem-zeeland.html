Tech Talent Ecosystem Zeeland

Connecting young people, education and industry
through meaningful technology experiences

A working exploration of regional collaboration, talent development and future-oriented technology education.

Why this case exists

Technical talent does not emerge automatically. Young people need opportunities to discover technology, experiment, create and experience its relevance.

The challenge is not only attracting students into technical education, but creating a continuous connection between children, schools, companies and society.

The challenge

Regional ecosystems often contain many valuable initiatives, but impact depends on connection: between educational levels, companies, public partners and the experiences offered to young people.

The question is not only: "How do we organise activities?"

The deeper question is: "How do we create a learning ecosystem where technology becomes visible, accessible and meaningful?"

The ecosystem perspective

Technology education becomes stronger when learning is connected to real environments, practical challenges and inspiring people.

  • Connecting primary education, secondary education and vocational pathways.
  • Creating meaningful encounters between students and technology.
  • Connecting regional companies with future talent.
  • Sharing experiences and building knowledge across initiatives.

Inspiration beyond borders

International examples show the value of strong links between learning, craftsmanship and regional identity.

From traditional craft exchange models to modern technology learning environments, the common principle is: people develop through participation, practice and connection.

The role of structure

Structure should not control creativity. It should create the conditions where collaboration, experimentation and learning can flourish.

A strong ecosystem combines:

  • Clear ambitions and shared direction.
  • Space for regional initiatives.
  • Knowledge exchange between partners.
  • Continuous learning from practice.

First hypotheses

  • Technology becomes attractive when young people can experience its meaning.
  • Companies should not only provide vacancies, but also become learning partners.
  • Regional collaboration requires structures that support trust and exchange.
  • Impact grows when successful experiences become reusable across the ecosystem.
Ecosystem Principles

Visibility

Making technology and opportunities visible for young people.

Connection

Bringing education, companies and society together.

Learning

Sharing experiences and continuously improving.
